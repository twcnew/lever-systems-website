## Always-on, not a one-off dump

A zip of last quarter’s posts is stale the week after you download it. The competitor ships a new carousel, shifts from TOF to hiring, tests a GIF format, puts a specialist on a topic the founder used to own. If your library does not move, you are strategizing on a corpse.

So the workflow has two speeds:

**Backfill, any duration.** You pick the accounts (company page + the people who actually post) and the lookback. Three months if you want a tight read. Six or twelve if you want rhythm, recycled plays, seasonal spikes. I pull text and assets, dedupe, tag funnel / format / visual / topic, and stand up the library.

**Live updates.** After that, each new post from those accounts is ingested, tagged, and indexed. Same filters. Same search. Same API for agents. You open the library on Monday and last week is already in there.

That is the product: always-on competitive intelligence on LinkedIn, for the competitors you name.

## What you can actually see

Captions to republish are useless. The system behind them is the asset.

- **Who posts.** Founder vs operators vs specialists. Who carries point of view, who carries product, who carries hiring.
- **Cadence and mix.** Enough history to see a rhythm, not a lucky week. Enough freshness to see when they break it.
- **Funnel.** TOF (point of view, lists, stories with no ask), MOF (how-to, stack, playbook, case), BOF (clear CTA, magnet, “book a call”).
- **Format.** Text-only, single image, GIF, carousel / PDF, multi-image.
- **Visual system.** Text cards, screenshots, personal branding, infographics, memes. The DA is in the files.
- **Angle families.** Outbound, tools, ads, deliverability, hiring, founder story. They repeat a family and drop another. That repetition is the strategy. A new family showing up is a strategy change.

A human searches it in the morning. An agent queries the same index instead of inventing a “LinkedIn post about GTM.”

## What you get out of it

**You stop starting from zero.** The category already spent the posts. Your first 30 do not have to be a guess. You occupy the angle families they proved, and the gaps they left (if everyone is on TOF carousels, MOF how-tos are empty).

**Your inbound looks like a company that knows the craft.** People still get the email. They still get the invite. Then they google you. A feed that matches how the category actually talks is trust. A feed of generic agent copy is a closed tab. This library is the reference so your drafts sit in the same league.

**Founders and content owners get hours back.** The alternative is scrolling LinkedIn for 20 minutes to “find inspiration” and leaving with nothing you can brief. Here you filter competitor + MOF + carousel + last 14 days and you have a pile.

**Writers and agents share one source of truth.** Briefs stop being vibes. The agent retrieves real posts, real hooks, real visual patterns, from this week, not from a prompt. You review. You rewrite in your voice. You ship.

![The library feeds the agent. Daily ideas, then you rewrite and ship.](/blog/ci-agent-loop.jpg)

**You see them move while they move.** A one-off scrape tells you who they were. Always-on tells you they just put three people on BOF, or they killed GIFs, or the specialist is now the volume account. You can answer that in the next sprint, not at the next quarterly offsite.

**You can copy the operating system, not the copy.** Cadence, roles, angle families, visual formats. Then you write *your* versions, in *your* DA. Several people posting, like a real GTM team, instead of one founder improvising on Sundays.

**It compounds with the rest of the motion.** Drafts into review, then Buffer / native, then site, ads, outbound. Same logic as the rest of Lever: signal in, next step out. The signal here is what the category is doing on LinkedIn, continuously.

## Where this sits in inbound

Inbound, in the way I wire it, is a trust layer that makes every other motion cheaper.

That is why I do not start inbound with a prompt. I start with a living base.

1. Backfill the competitors that matter, on the window you choose.
2. Keep the library current as they post.
3. Map the OS: who, how often, which angles, which visuals.
4. Rewrite in your voice. Feed the same index to the agent that helps you produce.
5. Route output into the stack you already pay for.

The library is the signal. The next step is a draft your founder can ship this week, informed by what went up yesterday.

## What I install

I do not hand over a zip of someone else’s images. I install the motion.

**Source.** The competitor set: company page plus the posters (founders, AEs, specialists). Lookback is yours. 30 days or 12 months. We can add a new competitor later without rebuilding the whole thing.

**Normalize.** Deduped posts, text, local assets (images, GIFs, carousels). Tags for funnel, format, visual type, topic. Manual overrides so a human can correct the heuristics.

**Always-on ingest.** New posts from those accounts land in the same library. Tags, search, agent API stay in sync.

**Library.** A small internal app: filters, search, a post page with the asset next to the copy. Daily use for the content owner. API for agents. This is what the video is.

**Playbook.** From the live corpus: who should post, how often, which angle families to occupy, which visual formats to keep. Then we write your versions.

**Handover.** It runs in your stack. I own the logic until your team can query the library, see a new competitor post on Monday, and ship without me in the loop.

I do not publish competitor copy. I do not train a public model on it. It is an internal intelligence layer, same class as win/loss notes or a battlecard, except it does not rot.
